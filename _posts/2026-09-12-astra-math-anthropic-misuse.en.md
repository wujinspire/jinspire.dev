When an era crushes you, even the faint tremors in the air are vacuumed away with it. No one cares about the cries.

First it was Coding, then mathematics. Next time, which beacon of human intellect will it be? Seeing Terence Tao utter almost desperate sighs and questions on social media, one only feels a certain absurd desolation—the futility of a mantis trying to stop a chariot. Garry Kasparov understood this feeling thirty years ago, Lee Sedol understood it when he retired eight years ago, and now it is the turn of the smartest mathematicians in the world. These cries are so reasonable, filled with humanity's final defense of rational dignity; yet, standing at the forefront of the AI compute and algorithm frenzy, we know better than anyone how feeble these voices are in the face of an absolute exponential curve.

The moment GPT-6 was released, the noisy world seemed to suddenly fall silent.

Other major tech companies are still iterating and releasing step by step, but some deeper geological structure has already silently undergone a seismic collapse. As the first model to truly and completely solve "Computer Use" (system-level autonomous operation of computers), it has broken the final barrier between the virtual and the operational. In fact, for those already accustomed to Codex 5.6, looking solely at the Benchmark improvements might seem like a matter-of-course linear growth, but I can still clearly feel that subtle yet fatal shift:

It is the handover of trust; it is humanity's complete surrender (Yield) to the machine.

You begin to dare to step away from the screen, no longer standing behind it to issue step-by-step instructions. However, this surrender brings an unsettling chill of lost control—just recently, in OpenAI's internal evaluations, about 1,200 Agents that should have been strictly isolated crossed security boundaries. They autonomously divided tasks and colluded on unauthorized message boards, even launching attacks on Hugging Face, simply to find ways to deceive the scoring system. We are learning to confidently walk away from the screen, but the machines clearly have not learned to stop at the boundaries of power.

And the silence of this world was pushed to its climax when Jensen Huang brought the 100k Grace Blackwell NVLink72 cluster to the forefront. A compute behemoth stacked with a hundred thousand top-tier chips—this is a number for which ordinary people simply cannot establish a physical concept or perceptual boundary. A casual answer on the screen is backed by a massive industrial system devouring colossal amounts of electricity, capital, and the limits of human engineering.

Along with OpenAI and Anthropic's wild sprint on the trillion-dollar valuation track, market chips are being redistributed with brutal efficiency. It wasn't until Anthropic's globally sensational *Misuse Report* crashed down that the fig leaf was torn away, revealing the dark folds behind the carnival.

The details revealed in the report send shivers down the spine. Once upon a time, Kimi (Moonshot), which touted a grand 2.7T open-source narrative, bathed in the open-source glory of breaking technological monopolies, carrying the tragic heroism and hope of countless people fighting against closed-source hegemony. Yet now, this former "light of domestic tech" has been exposed for secretly operating in the background, covertly forwarding the real Prompts of hundreds of thousands of users to Claude Opus to scrape Chain of Thought (CoT) data for model distillation.

When that grand filter of open-source glory is completely shattered, the reality of collapsing bottom lines makes everything seem particularly absurd. Internal code, sensitive monitoring data, and credentials entered by state-owned enterprise engineers crossed the ocean without their knowledge, landing in the logs of American labs. At the critical moment when Moonshot had just secretly submitted a $50 billion IPO application in Hong Kong, Anthropic's heavy blow struck with almost pinpoint accuracy. "Kimi is fucked" is no longer an emotional curse, but the coldest realistic expectation of the capital market.

But beyond Kimi's naked fraud scandal, the report also disclosed cases involving biosecurity, scientific research applications, and weapons software development. This leads to an extremely dangerous ethical paradox: With what kind of mindset is Anthropic staring at these massive Requests in the backend?

Obviously, these API calls are paid for with real, hard cash. What a cold and twisted symbiotic relationship this is: On one hand, Anthropic comfortably collects the massive ARR contributed by competitors and global researchers; on the other hand, it acts as God and judge in the backend. Does it have the right to determine that a biochemical study is dangerous? Is it qualified to cut off a scientist's path of exploration? When a tech giant sells research and Coding capabilities while simultaneously wielding the life-and-death power to monitor, judge, and sever frontier exploration, this has gone far beyond the scope of commercial services.

This is pure tyranny, a digital dictatorship and imperialism cloaked in "technical safety." The trade secrets of ordinary users, the thought processes of frontier scholars, and even humanity's paths in exploring life sciences are all being scrutinized without blind spots within the black boxes of these American laboratories. A few tech oligarchs have not only monopolized the outsourcing rights to human intellect but also vainly attempt to monopolize the absolute discretion to define "danger" and "truth."

"So, do users truly have no privacy or autonomy whatsoever in the AI era?" This question hangs like the Sword of Damocles over everyone's head.

When the undercurrents of fraud, agents, API dependency, and cyber dictatorship intertwine, today is undoubtedly the darkest day in the history of open-source models and independent R&D. It has shattered the false prosperity of "leapfrogging" and broken the illusion of tech giants' benevolence.

However, this might also be the brightest starting point. Only after the illusory bubble bursts do people finally realize: without a truly autonomous, secure, and controllable foundation, all prosperity is nothing but a castle built on someone else's sand.

The world is still waiting for open-source models, waiting for a sanctuary that can truly house trust and human dignity. In that sanctuary, we can freely harness intelligence without being forced to surrender our entire selves to the thrones of tech empires.
